Recorded Outbreak Activity and Reactive OCV Requests

Emergency access to the global oral cholera vaccine stockpile requires a formal request before approval and delivery.

01 · Background

Usual recorded activity or increases within a country?

Whether reactive requests are associated with countries' usual level of recorded outbreak activity or with increases within a country over time remains unclear. We examined these distinct associations across 25 African countries.

The request is a distinct stage before approval, shipment, and vaccination. The analysis estimates associations; it does not link individual outbreaks to requests or measure outbreak-to-request delay.

02 · Methods

A country-month analysis of recorded outbreak activity

We linked recorded cholera outbreak intervals with OCV requests in a country-month panel from July 2014 to December 2019. For each country-month, we summed the number of recorded outbreaks active in each of the preceding 12 months, yielding a measure of outbreak-months rather than case counts.

We log-transformed this measure and separated each country's average from monthly deviations around that average. The outcome was at least one dashboard-classified reactive request in a month.

Primary model

Logistic regression with between-country and within-country components included together.

Inference

Country-clustered asymptotic 95% confidence intervals and wild cluster bootstrap p-values.

Robustness

Predictor windows of 1, 3, 6, and 12 months across 25-, 23-, and 22-country samples.

Sensitivity

Exploratory models added baseline country-level covariates separately.

reactive request-months had no recorded active outbreak-months in the preceding 12 calendar months.

Countries with higher average recorded outbreak activity had more months with a reactive OCV request. We did not detect an association between monthly deviations from a country's usual outbreak activity and reactive requests. This result does not establish the absence of within-country responsiveness.

The between-country estimate remained positive but attenuated when DR Congo, South Sudan, and Nigeria were excluded and remained positive after separate covariate adjustments.

04 · Interpretation

Recorded activity is not the same as information available in real time

Reactive request-months were associated with persistent differences in recorded outbreak activity between countries; a corresponding within-country association was not detected.

Incomplete temporal overlap between recorded outbreak activity and reactive request-months limits interpretation of the within-country result. The recorded outbreak-activity series should not be assumed to reproduce the information available to a ministry of health when a request was prepared.

  • The analysis cannot link a request to the outbreak or location it was intended to address.
  • Country-month aggregation can conceal highly localized cholera activity.
  • The panel contains only 25 country clusters and 58 reactive request-months.
  • The estimates are associations; they are not causal.
  • The 2014–2019 findings should not automatically be generalized to the later stockpile environment.
