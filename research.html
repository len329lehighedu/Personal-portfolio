Reactive oral cholera vaccine requests are more strongly associated with recorded outbreak activity between countries than within countries

Emergency access to the global oral cholera vaccine stockpile requires a formal request. We examined whether reactive requests are associated with average outbreak activity across countries or deviations within countries.

This page presents a concise summary of the manuscript submitted to Vaccine. The article is under review; a publication link will be added if it becomes available.

01 · Background

Average activity across countries or deviations within countries?

Request submission is distinct from approval, shipment, and vaccination. We examined whether reactive requests were associated with countries' average recorded outbreak activity or with months when activity deviated from a country's own average.

The analysis estimates associations, not outbreak-to-request delay. Recorded activity also needs to be distinguished from underlying disease burden and from the information available when authorities consider vaccination.

02 · Methods

A country-month analysis of recorded outbreak activity

We linked outbreak records and OCV requests for 25 African countries during July 2013–December 2019. The analysis covered July 2014–December 2019, allowing a 12-month lookback and yielding 1,650 country-months.

The predictor summed monthly counts of active outbreak records during the preceding 12 months, excluding the outcome month. We separated each country's average logged activity from monthly deviations around that average. The outcome was at least one dashboard-classified reactive request in a month.

Primary model

Logistic regression with between-country and within-country components included together.

Inference

Country-clustered asymptotic 95% confidence intervals and wild cluster bootstrap p-values.

External audit

Public reports were reviewed for every reactive request-month with no preceding recorded activity.

Robustness

Multiple predictor windows, country samples, leave-one-country-out models, and sensitivity analyses.

03 · Results

17 of 22

audited request-months had external reports documenting cholera activity within the same country and preceding window.

The difference between the between- and within-country coefficients was 0.410 (95% CI [0.170, 0.651]; bootstrap p = 0.022), supporting a stronger association between countries than within countries.

Between-country odds-ratio intervals excluded 1 in all 25 leave-one-country-out analyses. Within-country intervals included 1 across sensitivity analyses.

04 · Interpretation

Recorded activity is not the same as information available in real time

Reactive requests were associated with average recorded activity across countries; a within-country association was not detected. The audit showed that zero activity in the linked series was not equivalent to the absence of documented cholera.

Most supporting reports did not identify the specific outbreak or area that motivated a request. The audit identifies differences in what the records capture, not necessarily errors in individual records.

  • The analysis cannot generally link a request to the outbreak or location it was intended to address.
  • Country-month aggregation can conceal highly localized cholera activity.
  • The panel contains only 25 country clusters and 58 reactive request-months.
  • The estimates are associations; they are not causal.
  • The 2014–2019 findings should not automatically be generalized to the later stockpile environment.

05 · My contribution

From data reconstruction to the submitted manuscript

As first author, I led data curation, formal analysis, investigation, visualization, and the original manuscript draft. I reconstructed the country-month panel from 576 outbreak records and 88 OCV requests, implemented the statistical workflow, and produced the figures and robustness analyses.

I also reviewed external documentation for all 22 reactive request-months with zero recorded activity in the preceding year. Finding supporting reports for 17 turned the largest data limitation into a second empirical finding while preserving the limits of request-specific linkage.

Data and analysis

Panel reconstruction, between–within modeling, clustered inference, bootstrap tests, and sensitivity analyses.

Investigation

A source-by-source audit with explicit evidence categories, correspondence rules, and unresolved uncertainty.

Communication

First-author drafting, figure design, journal-format revisions, and integration of iterative research feedback.

Reproducibility

Derived datasets, coding documentation, analysis scripts, and plotting code released on Zenodo.
